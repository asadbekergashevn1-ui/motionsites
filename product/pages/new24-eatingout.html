KEEPING IT RUNNING  ·  25THE PLAN LEAVES THE HOUSE WITH YOU

Eating out, without the maths.

Restaurants, family tables and travel days don't break the system — because the system is one rule, and the rule fits in your pocket: anchor first, in the portion you can finish. Three situations, handled.
1
The restaurantorder like you own the place
✓Order a protein starter as your main — prawn cocktail, carpaccio, grilled halloumi. Starter-sized is your sized now.
✓Ask for the box when the plate arrives, not after. Half goes in before you start — tomorrow's anchor, zero willpower needed.
✓Grilled > fried, sauce on the side — the fat page, restaurant edition.
✓Eat the protein off the plate first — nobody at the table will even notice the order.
2
The family tablesame food, your order
✓You don't need a separate menu — serve yourself the meat or fish first, a palm-sized piece, and start there.
✓Small plate, big spoon confidence — a full small plate beats a half-empty big one, at the table most of all.
✓"I'm done" is a complete sentence. Fullness arrives fast now; honour it without a speech.
✓Dessert? Three bites of someone else's. Joy stays on the menu.
3
The road & the officeanchors that travel
✓Shop coolers are full of anchors: yogurt pots, cheese portions, milk, boiled-egg packs, cooked prawns.
✓The desk drawer kit: tuna tins, crackers, a shaker and a tub of powder — two sittings that never spoil.
✓Alarms still fire on the road — the clock doesn't know you're travelling. Answer it with whatever anchor is nearest.
✓Queasy travel day? Cold shake from any shop fridge — the queasy row works at 70 mph too.
THREE SENTENCES THAT DO ALL THE WORK — SAY THEM WORD FOR WORD
“Could I get the starter portion of that as my main, please?”
“Could you bring a takeaway box with the food? I'll box half straight away.”
“Grilled if you can, and the sauce on the side — thank you.”
