Texture Ladder

Match the food to today, not to the plan.
Thin, cold, watery food leaves the stomach faster than thick, warm, fatty food. That is why a shake goes down on a day when chicken will not. The ladder is not about willpower — it is about picking a texture your stomach can actually move today, so the protein still gets in.

HOW TO CLIMB BACK UP
Test with one small portion from the rung above — two or three spoons, no more. If it sits comfortably for two hours, move up at the next meal, not the same one. If it does not, drop back and try again tomorrow. Nothing is lost by waiting a day.
WHAT NEVER CHANGES
Protein goes in first, on every rung. Fluids are easier between meals than with them. And on every rung there is still a number to hit — a lower one, but never zero.

After surgery, a texture ladder only goes one way.
On a GLP-1 it goes both ways — down on a dose week, back up as it settles.
Each rung shows how much of your target that texture can still reach.
Dropping a rung is the plan working, not the plan failing.
If you cannot hold fluids down for 24 hours, or you are passing much less urine than usual, stop and call your clinician.
