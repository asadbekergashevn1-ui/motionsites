Dose Week Mode

The week after a dose increase is predictable. So plan it.
Side effects are most likely when you start a GLP-1 or step up a dose, and they usually ease within one to two weeks. That is the whole point: this week is not a failure, it is a known pattern — and a known pattern can be planned for instead of survived.

WHAT CHANGES THIS WEEK
↓Fat. It slows your stomach further, on top of what the drug already does.
↑Fluids. Between meals, never with them — they take the space you need.
↓Fibre, for the first few days only. Put it back from Day 4 so you don't trade nausea for constipation.
×Alcohol. It makes nausea and reflux worse while you are adjusting.
×Large portions. Vomiting on a GLP-1 usually comes from volume, not from the food itself.
↑Frequency. A small breakfast, then something small every 3–4 hours.
STOCK THESE BEFORE DOSE DAY — YOU WILL NOT FEEL LIKE SHOPPING ON DAY 1
L1 · DAYS 1–2
  • Clear or isolate protein powder
  • Unsweetened almond or skim milk
  • Ice — cold is tolerated better
  • Electrolyte sachets
L2 · DAYS 3–4
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 0%
  • Cottage cheese
  • Smooth soup you can fortify
  • Silken tofu
L3 · DAYS 5–6
  • Eggs
  • Tinned tuna or salmon
  • Minced chicken or turkey
  • Soft white fish
L4 · DAY 7
  • Back to your normal green list
  • Fibre: berries, oats, pulses
  • Something you actually enjoy
The target drops. It never goes to zero.
Eating too little makes nausea worse, which makes you eat even less — that loop is the real risk of a dose week.
You can ask your prescriber about a slower titration. Never change your dose on your own.
Vomiting or diarrhoea for more than 24 hours, or much less urine than usual: call your clinician.
