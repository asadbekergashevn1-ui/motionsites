When Protein
Turns On You

Aversion is not nausea. It needs a different fix.
Nausea is your stomach. Aversion is your head — food that was fine last week is suddenly repulsive, even on a day your stomach feels normal. It hits hardest at the start and after a dose step, and it lands on protein more often than anything else. The fix is not willpower. It is changing one variable at a time until something slips past.
CHANGE ONE THING, NOT EVERYTHING
AXIS 1
Warm → Cold
Heat carries smell, and smell is usually what turns you. Cold food barely registers.
AXIS 2
Thick → Thin
Thick and creamy sits heavy and reads as "too much" before the first sip is down.
AXIS 3
Sweet → Savoury
Most protein products are sweet. Sweetness fatigue is real, and it arrives fast.
IF THIS IS OFF TODAYTRY ONE OF THESE INSTEAD
Sweet protein shakesthe most common one→ Cottage cheese with salt and pepper · unflavoured isolate stirred into cold soup · chilled savoury broth with milk powder
Chicken and warm meatthe smell gets there first→ Cold shredded chicken straight from the fridge · tinned tuna · cold prawns · sliced turkey
Yogurt and dairytexture, usually→ Silken tofu blended smooth · egg whites · clear isolate in iced water
Anything thickit reads as too much→ One scoop in 350 ml of iced water, sipped over an hour · frozen into cubes and chewed slowly
Eggssulphur smell→ Egg whites only · cold hard-boiled with lemon and salt · cottage cheese instead
Everything, all of itthe bad days→ Drop to L1 and stop trying to solve it. Clear isolate, iced, sipped. 60% of target still counts as a win.

3
Try three swaps from the row above before you decide protein is off entirely. Change one axis each time — temperature, then texture, then flavour. Most people find one that slips past on the second or third try.
WHAT GOT PAST ME TODAY
Rotate before you quit.
Most aversions are specific, not total — and most of them pass within two weeks.
If nothing at all goes down for 24 hours, that is no longer aversion. Call your clinician.
