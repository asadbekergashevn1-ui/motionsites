THE FOOD PAGES  ·  10SAME PROTEIN, LIGHTER DELIVERY

When fat is the problem,
change the cooking, not the food.

Fat is the slowest thing a stomach handles — and your medicine has already slowed that stomach down. Put the two together and a fried meal can sit for hours: heaviness, reflux, queasiness. The fix is rarely eating less protein. It is carrying the same protein in a lighter vehicle.
WHAT FAT DOES
Fat holds food in the stomach longest of the three macros — useful for a normal appetite, a burden on a slowed one.
WHAT THE MEDICINE DOES
It already delays emptying. A greasy meal stacks delay on delay — that is the heaviness hours later.
WHAT YOU DO
Keep the protein. Boil, poach, grill or bake it instead of frying, and skip the cream-based sauces on hard days.
BE STRICTEST ABOUT THIS WHEN…
Your first weeks on the medicine Every dose week Reflux or heaviness after meals You're over 60 — digestion slows with age anyway Any history of gallbladder trouble — and tell your prescriber

The humble boiled egg is the quiet hero of this whole page. Cooked in water, nothing added, 6 g each, fine cold, fits every rung of the texture ladder except the worst days — and on those, mash it soft with a fork and a little salt.
Honesty note: “fat slows gastric emptying” is established physiology, and the 2025 joint Advisory lists fatty foods among the common early triggers. The specific swaps are our practical translations, not a clinical protocol. Persistent pain after fatty meals — especially under the right ribs — is a call-your-prescriber matter, not a swap-harder matter: see Red Flags.
