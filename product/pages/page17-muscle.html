20–50%
OF WEIGHT LOST CAN BE
LEAN MASS, NOT FAT

The scale can't tell muscle from fat.
It all reads as “down.”

In published research on these medication classes, a large share of lost weight can come from lean body mass. Muscle is the thing that climbs your stairs, carries your shopping, and burns calories at rest — losing it is how weight loss ends up feeling like weakness, and how the weight returns faster if the medicine ever stops.
LEVER ONE — YOU'RE ALREADY PULLING IT
Hit the protein number
That is what pages 5–10 are for. Protein is the raw material; without it there is nothing for muscle to rebuild with. This page is why the number is non-negotiable.
LEVER TWO — THE OTHER HALF
Give muscle a reason to stay
The body keeps what it uses. Resistance work 2–3 times a week is the signal that says “still needed” — without it, protein alone fights with one hand.
THE MINIMUM THAT COUNTS — HOME, NO EQUIPMENT, ~15 MINUTES
1Sit-to-stand
From a firm chair, arms crossed. Stand, sit, repeat. The single most useful movement you own.
2Wall push-up
Hands on the wall, body straight, lower and press. Move to the counter as it gets easy.
3Doorway row
Hold the frame, lean back, pull yourself in. A resistance band does the same job better.
2–3 × 8–12
SETS × REPS, EACH MOVE
Two or three rounds of 8–12, stopping when the last two repetitions feel like work. Leave 48 hours before repeating the same muscles. When a move stops feeling like work, add resistance — a heavier band, a backpack with books. A 20–30 minute walk on the other days covers the rest.
WHY THIS LIVES IN A FOOD GUIDE
Because the two levers only work together, and dose week is where both get dropped at once — you eat less and move less in the same seven days. The dose-week tracker has a row for this: on hard weeks, don't add — just don't stop. Even one sit-to-stand session keeps the signal on.
Sources & scope: the 20–50% lean-mass share is from a published review of GLP-1 receptor agonist studies (Sargeant et al., 2019 — full citation on the Sources page); resistance 2–3×/week with 48 h recovery is standard strength guidance. Starting exercise with a heart condition, joint replacement, or after long inactivity is a check-with-your-clinician-first matter.
